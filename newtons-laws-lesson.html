Warm-up

The shopping cart problem

You push both carts with exactly the same force. The empty cart speeds up quickly. The full cart barely gets going.

Think about it: what is different about the two carts, and why would that change how fast they speed up?
Mass. The full cart has more mass, so the same push gives it a smaller acceleration. By the end of this lesson you will be able to calculate exactly how much smaller.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • 1State Newton's three laws of motion in your own words.
  • 2Use F = m × a to predict how force and mass change acceleration.
  • 3Identify the forces acting on an object in a free-body diagram.

Key takeaways

Law 1: objects resist changes in motion

Without a net force, a still object stays still and a moving object keeps moving at the same speed and direction.

Law 2: a = F ÷ m

Double the net force and the acceleration doubles. Double the mass and the acceleration halves.

Law 3: forces come in pairs

The pair acts on two different objects, so the forces do not cancel each other out.
